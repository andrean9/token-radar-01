Methodology

Implied value is the entry’s own claim, calibrated from the snapshot’s own figures — never a hardcoded list of names. For each category, the multiple is the median mcap/TVL ratio among token-bearing protocols in the snapshot that carry mcap data (at least 3 samples, else the global median). Then:

implied value = TVL × multiple × momentum × maturity × chains

  • Value (log scale, dominant): TVL × multiple. Size decides the rank first; the other factors only reorder near neighbours.
  • Momentum (weight 0.4): 1 + 0.4 × 7-day change, capped at ±50%. A +50% week lifts value 20%; a missing change means zero momentum.
  • Maturity (0.9–1.1): 0.9 + 0.2 × days since listing, capped at 3 years. Older, large, still-tokenless protocols score higher; a missing listing date is neutral, never zero.
  • Chains (capped): +2% per extra chain, up to 6 chains (+10% max).

The score (0–100) is the log of the implied value rescaled between the board’s min and max, so ranking by score and by implied value always agree. A protocol “records no token” when DefiLlama records neither a CoinGecko id nor a symbol for it — the record is not proof that no token exists.

Why the median, not the mean

The ratios the multiples come from are violently skewed: across the 128 token-bearing protocols with mcap data, the global mean mcap/TVL is 5.0386 against a median of 0.2138 — the mean is 24× the median. One protocol’s ratio reaches 400.20, and 36 of the 128 ratios sit above 1. A mean multiple would let a handful of tiny-TVL, high-mcap outliers set the price of a whole category: CEX would be priced at 7.8246× instead of 0.0748×, Basis Trading at 5.6424× instead of 0.6544×, Lending at 1.8349× instead of 0.1324×. The median is the middle protocol’s ratio — it moves only when the typical protocol in the category moves, and it is the value a new token in that category would most plausibly resemble. Sensitivity test — one modelling choice changed at a time

The ranking was re-run four times, changing a single choice per run. The mean multiple is the only choice that matters: it moves 194 of 198 rows by a median of 26 ranks and drops the rank correlation to 0.79. The three small factors are nudges, as designed — switching any one off leaves the top 10 untouched and moves the median row by 1–2 ranks.
