Methodology

Why the median, not the mean

The mcap/TVL ratios are wildly skewed. Across the 128 token-bearing protocols that calibrate the model, the global mean ratio is – while the median is – and the largest single ratio is –. A mean would let a handful of outliers set every category's multiple. The CEX category shows the same skew from the other side: mean – against a median of –. The median is the typical protocol; the mean is the loudest one.

The model

  • Multiple. Per category, the median mcap/TVL of its token-bearing protocols (at least 3 samples, otherwise the global median). One shared predicate decides which protocols calibrate, for the table below and for the diagnostics alike.
  • Implied value. TVL × multiple, adjusted by momentum (1 + 0.4 × 7-day change, capped at ±50%), maturity (0.9 + 0.2 × days since listing / 1095, capped at 3 years; a missing listing date is neutral), and chain diversification (+0.02 per extra chain, capped at +0.10).
  • Score. log10 of the implied value, stretched 0–100 across the board. Rank follows implied value.

Sensitivity — stability, not accuracy

Re-ranking with one assumption changed at a time. The mean multiple is the only load-bearing choice; momentum, maturity and chain count are small nudges. This measures how stable the ranking is under changed assumptions. It does not prove the ranking predicts future launches.

Limits of this model

  • No shrinkage: a category with only 3 samples uses its median directly, and small categories are noisier for it.
  • No out-of-sample predictive validation: nothing here proves this ranking predicts which protocols will launch a token, or what it will be worth.
  • Sensitivity is stability, not accuracy: a ranking can be stable under changed assumptions and still be wrong.
  • Share of implied value is a share of this board's total. It is not a probability of a token launch.
